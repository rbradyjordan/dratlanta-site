Shade & smile design

Matched to you.
Not to a chart.

Most veneer shades get picked off a generic chart designed around one kind of face. Dr. Ennuson designs shade, value, and translucency around yours — because the same porcelain reads completely differently against different skin.

Shoot: shade tabs against skin — macro, warm grade
The method

Three decisions most providers skip.

01

Undertone, not just shade

Warm, cool, or neutral undertones change how the same porcelain reads. A bright bleach shade that flatters one face can read chalky and flat against deeper skin. We test shade tabs against your skin in natural light, not just against your teeth.

02

Value and translucency

Natural teeth aren't opaque. Porcelain that's too sheer goes dull and gray against melanin-rich skin; too opaque goes denture-flat. We specify the translucency layer by layer with the lab — that's what "custom" is supposed to mean.

03

The gum line matters

Margin placement and gingival contrast are part of the design, not an afterthought. We plan how the porcelain meets your gum tissue so the transition disappears — and we photograph every case to hold ourselves to it.

Photography-led

The lab sees what we see.

Every case is documented with high-resolution, color-calibrated photography — full face, smile, and macro — so the ceramist isn't guessing from a shade code on a form. It's the difference between porcelain made for a chart and porcelain made for a person.

The result gallery shows this across real patients of every skin tone — same lighting, same angles, no filters.

See cases across skin tones

Try it before it's real

You approve the design first.

Shade decisions are made with you, in daylight, against your skin — then proven in a wax-up preview and trial temporaries before any final porcelain is fabricated. If the value is wrong in real life, we change the design, not your expectations.
