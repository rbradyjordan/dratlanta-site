The licensed care guide

Anyone can buy porcelain.
Not everyone should touch your teeth.

Atlanta has learned this the hard way: "veneer specialists" operating without a dental license, working out of studios, leaving people with damaged enamel, failing gums, and no recourse. Here's what a license actually protects you from — and how to check anyone's, including ours.

What the license means

Veneers are a medical procedure.

01

Diagnosis before design

A licensed dentist takes X-rays and checks gum health, decay, and bite before any cosmetic plan. Unlicensed operators skip straight to gluing porcelain onto whatever is there — including infection and decay that then progress underneath.

02

Anesthesia, sterility, standards

Local anesthesia, sterilized instruments, and clinical standards are legal requirements of a dental practice. Outside one, none of that is guaranteed — and preparation without proper anesthesia is how enamel gets butchered.

03

Accountability

A licensed dentist answers to the Georgia Board of Dentistry, carries malpractice coverage, and has a license to lose. When unlicensed work goes wrong, there is no board, no coverage, and usually no one left to call.

Red flags

Six questions to ask anyone offering veneers.

  1. Are you a licensed dentist in this state? (Verify at the Georgia Board of Dentistry's license lookup — takes two minutes.)
  2. Will you take X-rays and examine my gums before quoting me?
  3. Is this porcelain from a dental lab, or composite applied in one sitting?
  4. What happens if something fails in a year — who fixes it, under what coverage?
  5. Can I see full cases — before and after, same lighting — not just afters?
  6. Where exactly is your practice? (A real practice has a fixed address you can walk into. Pop-ups and hotel-room appointments are the biggest red flag there is.)
If it already went wrong

Correction work, without judgment.

A meaningful part of Dr. Ennuson's caseload is correcting veneer work done cheaply, quickly, or outside a license — here and overseas. If that's you, you won't get a lecture. You'll get X-rays, an honest assessment of what's salvageable, and a written plan with a real price.

Correction cases are more complex than first-time veneers, and we'll be straight with you about what's involved. What we won't do is make it worse to make it fast.
