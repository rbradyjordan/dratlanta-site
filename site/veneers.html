Porcelain vs. composite

Why porcelain.

Composite veneers are sculpted chairside from filling material — faster and cheaper, but they stain, chip, and typically need replacing in 4–7 years. Porcelain is fabricated in a dental lab from your approved design, resists stain, holds its polish, and typically lasts 10–15 years with proper care.

We place porcelain because we plan smiles in decades, not seasons. If composite is genuinely the right call for your case, we'll tell you that too.

Plain answers

The questions everyone actually asks.

Do veneers hurt?

Preparation is conservative and fully numbed — most patients describe pressure, not pain. Some sensitivity for a few days afterward is normal and settles on its own. You leave with temporaries the same day, so you're never walking around mid-treatment.

How long do porcelain veneers last?

10–15 years is typical with proper care. What shortens that: night grinding without a guard, nail biting, opening packages with your teeth. What extends it: a night guard, normal brushing and flossing, and regular cleanings. We build the bite so force is distributed — longevity is designed in, not hoped for.

What if I hate them?

You'll never be surprised by your own smile. Before any porcelain is made you approve a wax-up preview, then wear trial temporaries shaped like the final design in your real life — talking, eating, photos. The design is revised until you approve it. Porcelain is only fabricated from a design you've already lived with.

Will they look fake?

Bad veneers look fake for two reasons: one flat shade of white, and shapes that ignore the face around them. Dr. Ennuson matches shade and translucency to your skin undertone and designs shapes from your facial photography — the goal is a smile people notice without being able to say why.

Do you shave the teeth down to pegs?

No. That's a hallmark of aggressive overseas work. Preparation here is conservative — only what's needed for the porcelain to fit and bond for the long term. Preserving your natural tooth structure is what makes veneers repairable and replaceable decades from now.

What does it cost?
